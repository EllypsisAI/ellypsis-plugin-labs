# Structures — the variants

There is no single correct carousel shape. Two beat sequences and six archetypes are
carried here because all of them have worked. Pick per piece; do not default to a
favourite.

Order of decision: **outcome → archetype → beat sequence → slide plan.**
Picking the shape from the piece instead of the outcome is the common failure — the
piece will always suggest whatever shape its author was already in.

## 1. Outcome first

Name the outcome before anything else: saves, shares, comments, DMs, or follows. Each
archetype reliably wins a different one. Posting a confession and wondering why nobody
saved it is not a quality problem, it is a format problem.

If the user has not named an outcome, infer it from the piece and say which one you
inferred in one line. Do not spend a question on it — that is what the three-question
budget is for, and this one is inferable.

## 2. The six archetypes

| # | Archetype | Alias | What it is | Primary win |
|---|-----------|-------|------------|-------------|
| 1 | **The System** | the Playbook | Your whole process, numbered, with one rule you never break | **Saves** — nobody can hold six rules in their head |
| 2 | **The Formula** | the Blueprint | One skill broken into *named* parts | **Saves + authority** — naming makes it yours, not recycled advice |
| 3 | **The Teardown** | the Autopsy | Before and after, with exactly one variable changed | **Shares** — people send it to someone who needs it |
| 4 | **The Vault** | the Collection | A numbered collection (17 skills, 20 formats, 50 hooks) | **Highest save rate of any type** — impossible to remember, so it must be stored |
| 5 | **The Template** | the Fill-In | Not a post they read, a thing they use | **Comments + DMs** — using it means asking you for it |
| 6 | **The Confession** | the Scar | An honest admission with the lesson attached | **Follows + comments — explicitly NOT saves** |

The archetype is a contract with the algorithm, not a stylistic preference.

**The Confession has a gate — scar, not wound.** Share from a scar: healed, with the
takeaway attached. Never a wound that is still bleeding. Ask of the piece: is there a
transferable lesson here, or is this bleeding on the page? No lesson yet means it is a
journal entry — say so and hold it rather than building slides around it.

## 3. Beat sequence A — five beats

Tight, and built around a giveaway. Every slide in it is doing one job.

1. **HOOK** — contrarian one-liner, huge type, zero context, no explanation.
   Patterns observed: "Nobody talks about this effect." / "Everyone uses ChatGPT."
   (then subverts it) / "It looks human. It's not."
2. **TENSION** — why the obvious approach fails, what the reader has been doing wrong.
   One idea per slide, max two supporting sentences. Numbered steps if procedural
   ("Step 1: The Fonts").
3. **PROOF** — before/after, "Same image. Completely different feel.", real outputs
   fanned out in a physical arrangement (prints on a table, posters overlapping). The
   proof is always *shown*, never claimed.
4. **THE GIFT** — the actual usable asset, presented as a ready-to-take object rather
   than as text on a slide. The gift is what gets the save; saves drive
   distribution.
5. **CTA CLOSE** — urgency + save/comment trigger: "Save this. It ages so fast. Half of
   these were free secrets a year ago. The window's closing."

Beats 2 and 3 usually run over more than one slide. Beats 1, 4 and 5 are one slide each.

**A has no gift?** Then A is the wrong sequence. Beat 4 is load-bearing — a five-beat
carousel with an empty gift slot is four beats and a shrug. Use B.

## 4. Beat sequence B — six beats

Told like a story rather than written like a list. The spine in three acts:
**Setup = slides 1–2, Tension = slides 3–5, Payoff = slide 6.**

### Beat 1 — THE COLD OPEN
Open at the most interesting moment, then back up. Do not introduce the topic — drop
them inside it.
- ✗ "I want to talk about storytelling."
- ✓ "I deleted 40,000 followers on purpose."

The first slide isn't a title, it's the cliff edge. Visual job: maximum contrast,
minimum words, one image that raises a question.

### Beat 2 — THE MIRROR
Say the sentence they have thought but never admitted out loud. The second they see
themselves, they stop scrolling and start caring. Shape:
*"You've saved 200 carousels and posted none of them."*

**Test:** if they don't feel *caught*, you haven't mirrored them yet.
Visual job: quiet, roomy, one subject — nothing competing with the recognition.

### Beat 3 — THE SPINE
Every slide must end **owing the next slide a reason to swipe.** A carousel is a
sequence, and the order is the argument.

**Test — the strongest single test in the set:** cover any slide. If the next one still
makes sense without it, your spine is broken.
Visual job: an order the eye can feel — numbering, progression, escalating density.

### Beat 4 — THE RHYTHM
Change the **visual weight** every slide so the swipe never goes flat. One-word slide,
then a dense one. A full-bleed image. A colour flip. The story is in the design too.
Same energy on every slide reads as one long slide.
Failure mode: uniform, "consistent" slides — pleasant and forgettable.

Rhythm is the one beat that is not a slide. It governs the whole set — see
`composition.md`.

### Beat 5 — THE TURN
Earn the lesson, then make it theirs. First a small honest admission so they trust you,
then the takeaway.

**Rewrite rule, apply it literally:** never *"here's what I learned."* Always
*"so you can ___."* Shape: *"I wasted two years on this so you can skip to the part
that works."*

This is the slide they screenshot — make it the sharpest line in the set.

### Beat 6 — THE PAYOFF
Flip a belief, then give one specific action. Close on the realisation that makes the
offer feel *necessary, not optional*. Two required parts: (a) the belief shift, (b) a
single next step.
- Weak close: "follow for more."
- Strong close: "comment FORMULA and I'll send you the template."

A carousel that doesn't close is just content; one that closes is a funnel.

**The comment-to-get-it close is opt-in and off by default.** It is largely an American
move and reads as try-hard elsewhere. Offer it only when the piece has an actual asset
to give, and only as the third question in the budget. Everywhere else, close on the
belief shift plus a plain next step.

## 5. Choosing between A and B

| The piece is… | Use |
|---|---|
| A method, a list, a teardown — and it ends in something usable | **A**, five beats |
| An argument, an experience, a change of mind | **B**, six beats |
| Both — a story that produces a tool | **B**, with A's gift slotted at beat 5's position |
| Neither, a rough idea with no shape yet | **B**. It survives thin material better; A's proof and gift beats both need substance the idea may not have |

Archetype narrows it further: Vault and Template want A. Confession wants B. System,
Formula and Teardown work in either — pick on whether the piece argues or lists.

Say which sequence you picked and why, in one line, at Gate 1. Do not make the user ask.

## 6. Rules that hold across every variant

- **The cover has one job: stop the scroll.** Not explain the topic. Not introduce you.
  Stop the thumb. Big type, high contrast, and either a promise or a number.
  **If the cover needs more than 8 words, they are already gone.**
- **Slide 2 confirms the hook or they leave.** The cover buys the swipe; slide 2 earns
  the rest of the set. Its job is to set up the problem and create the stakes. On
  landing at slide 2 the reader must immediately think *this is worth my time*. If not,
  the cover has retroactively become a lie.
- **Every slide must work as a standalone screenshot.** Sent to someone with zero
  context, it still lands. Enforcement: max two fonts, high contrast, **one idea per
  slide**, max two supporting sentences.
- **Every carousel needs one slide worth saving on its own.** A framework, a prompt, a
  fill-in-the-blank, or a line sharp enough to be quoted. This is the slide that
  converts reach into saves. In A that is the gift; in B it is usually the Turn. If no
  single slide is save-worthy, the carousel as a whole won't be either.
- **Attention is re-won, not won.** The hook buys about one second; after that the
  reader keeps asking *is this still worth my time?* Slide 2, the Spine and the Rhythm
  are the three enforcement mechanisms for that, at the copy, structure and visual
  layers respectively.
- **Content bleeds into the next slide's edge** — a fragment of the next image visible
  at the boundary. Swipe bait.
- **Slide numbers are always visible.**
